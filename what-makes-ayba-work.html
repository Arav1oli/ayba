What makes AYBA work

Not a logo on a wall — a working network of trusted brokers who list together, sell together, and hold each other to a shared standard.

24 July 2026 · About the AYBA

Plenty of industries have an association. Fewer have one that changes how the work actually gets done. The Australasian Yacht Brokers Association was built to be the second kind — an organisation that makes its members more effective, not just more credentialled.

So what actually makes it work? It comes down to four things: a network that sells together, commissions that are protected, a membership that's genuinely vetted, and a standard everyone signs up to.

1. A co-brokerage network

Today, a listing usually sits with one broker. On the AYBA model, your listing is visible to the whole trusted network — and when a fellow member brings the buyer, you split the commission fairly instead of losing the deal entirely. That single change turns competitors into a sales force working on your boats.

List once and reach every member. Fewer stale listings, cleaner deals, more boats sold.

It's a better outcome for the seller, who gets more eyes on their vessel, and a better outcome for the broker, who gets a wider pool of qualified buyers than any single office could ever reach alone.

2. Protected commissions

A united, professional membership keeps rates where they should be. When brokers hold a common standard, the whole market holds its value — and bargain-basement operators can't drag high-value boats down with them. That protection is worth real money on every transaction, and it's only possible when enough of the industry stands together.

3. A vetted, trusted circle

Membership is by application and review. That matters. It means you deal with brokers who won't go behind your back mid-deal — people of repute who do business the right way. The value of the AYBA badge is precisely that clients and fellow brokers can trust it. Let anyone in and the badge means nothing; be deliberately selective and it means everything.

  • Established, practising brokers of good standing.
  • A commitment to fair co-brokerage conduct — no cutting other members out of a deal.
  • Proper handling of client deposits and a willingness to operate to the AYBA Code of Practice.

4. A shared code and a common voice

Standardised agreements, best-practice guides and an agreed code of conduct protect everyone at the table — buyer, seller and broker alike. And a united membership gives the profession a genuine voice in the standards, contracts and regulation that shape it. Individually, brokers react to change. Together, they help write it.

Where it's heading

The association is building toward a single platform where members list, collaborate and transact — auto-synced listings, broker-only notes, and downloadable, brand-ready material that pushes straight to your database. The destination is simple: one central place for good brokers to work together, and a cleaner, stronger market for everyone in it.
